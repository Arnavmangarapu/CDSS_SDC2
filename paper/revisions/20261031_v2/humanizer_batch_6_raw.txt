We looked at a clinical review prototype that separates who the source is, missing patient details and fixed medication‑warning rules. We used 108 cases the same way we had before with the same proposals and label parts. When we compared the results the warning and support decisions matched the developer labels in all 108 cases for deterministic extraction plus rules. For LLM‑only it matched 81 out of 108. For production LLM extraction plus rules it matched 106 out of 108. For rule‑filtered LLM warnings it matched 83 out of 108. All failed attempts were still counted in the numbers. Filtering made the warning precision better. The recall dropped to 63.9 %. This shows that we must report the decisions even when there are wrong supports. In a study with 36 vignette diagnoses LLM‑only, text retrieval and graph plus text matched 18, 19 and 17 out of 36 respectively. No method showed a diagnostic advantage. Source‑identity controls accepted all 26 inputs and rejected all 132 altered inputs. These results show how the system behaves and recovers after fixing a bug on the development data. They do not prove semantic checking, clinical prescribing safety or independent generalization. The hybrid method did not make exact‑case performance better than the baseline.

LLM‑only wrongly supported 14 out of 51 warning statements that it gave. Filtering cut that to 0 out of 34. The useful support recall fell from 37 out of 48 to 34 out of 48. Removing warnings did not fix the missing ones. The hybrid had 0 out of 45 supported warnings and a useful recall of 45 out of 48 compared with 0 out of 48 and 48 out of 48 for deterministic rules. Two hybrid attempts failed: one timed out and one counted a request after the system stopped. One LLM‑only answer was invalid. Controlled scoring did not count a model extraction as a deterministic fallback; filter failures did not add extra model calls. Raw medication name and status sets matched 76 out of 108 cases; merged allergy status and current‑medication sets matched 106 out of 108 each. Raw allergy status was not scored because the unchanged production JSON schema does not ask for it. All 124 source phrases were copied exactly which does not prove correctness or that all statements were kept. These developer‑labeled regression results give no exact‑case advantage over the deterministic baseline.

The prototype gives source‑identity checks and fixed medication‑warning decisions while keeping uncertainty. On the development cases production extraction plus rules made more exact decisions than LLM‑only warnings but it did not exceed deterministic extraction plus rules. Post‑generation filtering reduced support at the cost of useful coverage. Changes in diagnosis history show recovery after a bug fix, without a proven graph advantage. These findings encourage independent case creation and clinical review before claiming generalization; they do not confirm clinical entailment or prescribing safety.
